# CS-350-Portfolio-Entry
Module 8 entry for CS 350 Emerging Sys Arc &amp; Tech

The two artifacts come to us from module five and seven respective, module five deals with using the LED's to communicate a message in Morse code, with the button switching between two different messages. Whereas module seven deals with a thermometer system, involving the usage of said thermal sensor for the temperature readings, and three buttons, one for switching between modes, and two others for the setpoint adjustment, which determines whether a metaphorical heater/ac should be on or off. I had felt that, for the most part, the hardware had been installed and the programming had been done to specifications, although I am particular to the latter, since I had translated the wiring on the instructions to where I had the buttons at. In regards to the software, the coding for both were using the in-house editor on the Raspberry Pi, which posed a issue or two at first, but would be an essential part of both assignments, which is a feat, given I hadn't even heard, nor used nano, up until this point. There are a few places that I could improve, mainly concerning places outside the software and hardware. For one, the graphs themselves, while at least on paper, could have been better made in both instances, both in the matter of size, and for a better understanding. Another factor is the writing in my questions, which frankly, is largely my own fault in running off a cliff at times with the answers. In any case, a good dose of avoiding runoff's is something that could improve that. Lastly is the videos, specifically the lighting. Frankly, it wasn't easy to get neither a good light, nor a good view of everything, especially the LCD, without saturating the area with lighting. This is one area where a proper investment into some half-decent lighting and especially a tripod or grip, or even a decent video recorder in that matters, would do wonders. in any case, the entirely of the Raspberry Pi, as well as nano, is something that will be added to my support list for the foreseeable future. Outside of this unit, I can ascertain that that little box will be getting some more mileage, especially in the coming future units, especially for its overall versatility, and probably will see a lot more usage outside of the professional area, with a lot of different setups to choose from. I did make the project readable, at least the one on module seven by keeping with the already established naming conventions of the application. I had also kept things maintainable by reusing existing functions, such as updateLights, reducing the need for duplicating code, and allowing for further modification without needing to rewrite the entire thing. Lastly, I had kept things adaptable by implementing the changes that were needed, based on the existing models, while keeping the overall design of the program intact.
